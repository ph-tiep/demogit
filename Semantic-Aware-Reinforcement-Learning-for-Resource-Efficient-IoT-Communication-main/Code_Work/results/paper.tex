\begin{abstract}
% ============================================================
Effective Internet of Things (IoT) network management requires simultaneous semantic feature
compression, real-time channel quality monitoring, anomaly detection, and
adaptive resource allocation. Existing approaches address these tasks in
isolation, lacking a unified system that jointly exploits temporal dynamics
and multi-agent coordination. In this paper, a three-stage framework is proposed that integrates
Variational Autoencoder (VAE)-based semantic compression, an Extended Kalman Filter (EKF)-based Digital Twin (DT) for joint
quality monitoring and anomaly detection, and Multi-Agent Deep Reinforcement Learning with Graph Attention
Networks (MADRL-GAT) for cooperative resource allocation.
Experiments on three heterogeneous real-world IoT datasets
including Sigfox Antwerp, LoRaWAN Italy, and Indoor, demonstrate that the proposed
EKF-based DT substantially outperforms unsupervised clustering baselines
including K-Means and Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) in quality classification and delivers competitive anomaly detection
against dedicated single-task detectors including Local Outlier Factor (LOF), Isolation Forest (IF), and Z-Score, while being the only evaluated method to simultaneously perform both tasks without labeled fault data. MADRL-GAT achieves stable policy convergence across all settings, and the full pipeline executes in approximately 1~ms per inference step on CPU, confirming real-time feasibility and generalizability across Sigfox, LoRaWAN, and WiFi deployments.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The explosive growth of IoT deployments across smart cities, industrial sensing, and indoor positioning places unprecedented demands on wireless network infrastructure~\cite{ref_iot_overview}. Raw sensor observations are high-dimensional and noisy, making downstream learning inefficient, while the wireless channel state evolves continuously, requiring real-time quality monitoring and fault awareness to guide resource management. Addressing both challenges simultaneously within a unified framework remains an open problem, as existing works tackle them in isolation~\cite{ref_vae_wireless,ref_drl_iot}. On the compression front, Liu et al.~\cite{ref_vae_wireless} propose an adaptable VAE encoder for task-oriented semantic compression that jointly optimizes encoding and resource allocation, achieving up to 80\% transmission data reduction without degrading task success on static IoT links. Their approach, however, targets fixed channel conditions without temporal quality tracking or anomaly detection capability, limiting its applicability to dynamic IoT environments.
On the resource management front, Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) provides an effective basis for adaptive resource management in IoT networks. Xiao et al.~\cite{ref_drl_comm2} formulate joint blocklength and power allocation for Internet-of-Controllable-Things devices as a decentralized partially observable Markov decision process. They solve it with a mixed-centralized-decentralized MADRL algorithm, achieving a 41.52\% energy efficiency gain over non-ML allocation schemes, yet the framework operates on raw control-state features without channel-quality feedback and provides no fault detection capability. In a single-agent formulation, Xiong et al.~\cite{ref_drl_iot} model IoT edge resource allocation as a Markov decision process and propose an improved Deep Q-Network (DQN) that achieves an average reward of $-6.91$ versus $-8.18$ for the time-priority baseline through load-balanced scheduling across heterogeneous devices. Their single-agent design does not exploit inter-device cooperation, and raw task features inflate the state space without encoding any channel-quality or fault information.
To incorporate channel dynamics into resource decisions, Tang et al.~\cite{ref_dt_resource} integrate a DT with distributed DRL for Industry~4.0 network slicing, where the DT maintains a virtual replica of network state to guide agent policy updates, improving slice admission rates by over 6\% and reducing average network latency by 50\% under bursty traffic. Their DT relies on fixed classification thresholds that cannot adapt to distribution shift and provides no anomaly detection capability. For joint quality and anomaly management, Wali et al.~\cite{ref_wali2025} combine a single-agent DQN with supervised ensemble classifiers, achieving an Area Under the Curve (AUC) of 1.0 and F1-score of 99.95\% on labeled benchmarks across heterogeneous IoT datasets. Their system, however, requires operator-provided signal-quality labels during training and cannot exploit multi-agent cooperative behavior, limiting scalability in label-scarce deployments.
The closest prior work, Hazarika et al.~\cite{ref_semadt}, represents the first attempt to jointly integrate VAE-based semantic compression, DT channel forecasting, and MADRL with a Graph Neural Network (GNN), reducing communication overhead by over 50\% while retaining survivor detection above 85\%. Their anomaly detector, however, relies on a scalar reconstruction threshold with no statistical guarantee on false-alarm rate, and evaluation is confined to a single maritime domain, leaving cross-technology generalization unverified.
To address these limitations, this paper proposes a unified three-stage framework that combines unsupervised semantic compression, statistically principled DT monitoring, and graph-attentive multi-agent resource allocation.

The VAE compresses raw observations into compact latent codes that serve as agent states. The EKF-based DT then tracks channel dynamics to jointly deliver quality classification and anomaly detection without labeled fault data. Finally, MADRL-GAT uses DT-derived quality signals as reward inputs and exploits attention-weighted inter-agent relationships for cooperative resource allocation.
The main contributions are as follows.
\begin{itemize}
\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}\setlength{\parsep}{0pt}
\item A unified three-stage pipeline integrating semantic compression, channel monitoring, anomaly detection, and cooperative resource allocation, validated across Sigfox, LoRaWAN, and WiFi.
\item An EKF-based DT achieving quality-classification AUC gains up to $+80.4\%$ over clustering baselines on LoRaWAN and anomaly AUC of 0.987 on Indoor, as the only evaluated method delivering both tasks simultaneously without labeled fault data.
\item A MADRL-GAT policy converging within 100 episodes, reaching maximum-feasible reward on LoRaWAN and Indoor, and outperforming DQN-noGAT by $+4.6\%$ on Sigfox Antwerp where inter-agent graph structure is informative.
\end{itemize}

\section{System Model}
\label{sec:model}
% ============================================================

\vspace{-7pt}
\subsection{IoT Network Channel Model}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/iotnodes.pdf}
  \caption{Heterogeneous IoT network model with three wireless technologies.}
  \label{fig:iotnodes}
\end{figure}

The system comprises $N$ IoT nodes, each transmitting raw observations $\mathbf{x}_i(t)\in\mathbb{R}^d$ (e.g., Received Signal Strength Indicator (RSSI), Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR), and sensor telemetry) over a wireless channel, where $d$ varies by technology (see Table~\ref{tab:config}). The channel state and unsupervised quality label are defined as
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{s}_i(t) = \bigl[{\rm RSSI}_i(t),\; n^{\rm BS}_i(t)\bigr]^\top, \quad
y_i(t) = \mathbf{1}\!\bigl[{\rm RSSI}_i(t) \geq \tau_{\rm med}\bigr],
\label{eq:channel}
\end{equation}
where $n^{\rm BS}_i(t)$ is the active base-station count and $\tau_{\rm med}$ is the per-dataset median RSSI, yielding approximately balanced binary labels without operator annotation. The DT-EKF subsequently refines classification by deriving a data-adaptive threshold $\tau^*$ via Youden's J~\cite{ref_youden} on the EKF-filtered output, correcting for the distribution shift introduced by smoothing (Section~\ref{sec:dt}).

\vspace{-7pt}
\subsection{Resource Allocation Model}

At each decision epoch, node $i$ selects a discrete action $a_i(t)\in\{1,\ldots,|\mathcal{A}|\}$ representing its resource configuration, such as transmission power level, spreading factor, or channel index. The joint goal is to maximize the long-term cumulative cooperative reward across all agents, as formalized in Section~\ref{sec:problem}.

\vspace{-7pt}
\subsection{Fault Model}

Real IoT deployments rarely provide labeled fault records, making supervised anomaly evaluation infeasible without synthetic injection. In this work, faults are injected at 5\% of each sequence: a rate low enough to reflect realistic rare-event prevalence yet high enough to yield sufficient positive samples for stable AUC estimation. The injection model is
\begin{equation}
\tilde{s}^{(1)}_i(t) = s^{(1)}_i(t) + \delta_t \cdot f_k\!\bigl(s^{(1)}_i\bigr), \quad y_t = \delta_t,
\label{eq:fault}
\end{equation}
where $\delta_t\!\sim\!\mathrm{Bern}(0.05)$ is the fault indicator and $f_k$ is the perturbation for fault type $k$. Three representative IoT sensor fault patterns are defined: Spike ($+2\sigma$) and Drop ($-2\sigma$) apply symmetric perturbations exceeding two standard deviations from the channel mean, placing injected faults in the tail region beyond the 95\% coverage interval to ensure they are detectable yet non-trivial, while Stuck ($+1.5\sigma$) uses a smaller persistent offset to reflect the gradual nature of sensor freeze. Labels $y_t$ are used solely for AUC evaluation, and the DT-EKF operates without label access.

\vspace{-5pt}
\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}
% ============================================================

The channel state model (Eq.~\ref{eq:channel}) and fault labels (Eq.~\ref{eq:fault}) together define the system inputs. The joint goal is to simultaneously minimize semantic distortion and maximize long-term cooperative reward. Solving for the VAE encoder $(\phi,\theta)$ and multi-agent policies $\{a_i\}$ jointly is computationally prohibitive, as the policy gradient depends on the latent codes, creating a circular dependency. The problem is therefore decomposed into a sequential two-phase paradigm as shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}.

\textit{Phase~1. Semantic compression.}
The VAE is trained offline to maximize the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO)~\cite{ref_vae}
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\rm VAE}(\phi,\theta)=
  \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi}\!\bigl[\log p_\theta(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{z})\bigr]
  - D_{\rm KL}\!\bigl(q_\phi(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})\,\|\,p(\mathbf{z})\bigr),
\label{eq:elbo}
\end{equation}
where the first term encourages faithful reconstruction and the KL term regularizes the latent space toward a standard Gaussian prior. Once converged, the encoder is frozen at $\phi^*$, providing a stable compact representation for all downstream stages.

\textit{Phase~2. Cooperative resource allocation.}
With latent codes fixed, per-agent policies are optimized via the decentralized problem
\begin{equation}
(\mathcal{P}_{\rm RL})\!:\;\max_{\{a_i\}}\; \mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{N} r_i(t) \;\Bigg|\; \hat{\mathbf{z}}_i(t)=\bm{\mu}_{\phi^*}(\mathbf{x}_i(t))\right]
\label{eq:rl_opt}
\end{equation}
where $r_i(t)$ is the per-agent reward (Section~\ref{sec:method}) and $a_i(t)\in\{1,\ldots,|\mathcal{A}|\}$. This decoupled formulation eliminates the encoder–policy circular dependency and directly motivates the three-stage pipeline in Section~\ref{sec:method}, where Stage~1 solves Phase~1 offline while Stages~2 and 3 jointly realize Phase~2 at runtime via DT-EKF channel monitoring and MADRL-GAT resource control.

\section{Proposed Framework}
\label{sec:method}
% ============================================================

\begin{figure*}[tp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/lưu_đồ_thuật_toán (3).pdf}
  \caption{Overview of the proposed framework.}
  \label{fig:framework}
\end{figure*}

Fig.~\ref{fig:framework} illustrates the overall architecture. The VAE
is pre-trained offline, and at runtime the frozen encoder projects each
observation $\mathbf{x}_i(t)$ to a semantic latent code
$\hat{\mathbf{z}}_i(t)$ that feeds the MADRL agents. Concurrently, the
observation sequence $\mathbf{s}_i(t)$ drives the DT-EKF, whose
quality labels shape the agent reward signal. The three stages are detailed
below.

\vspace{-6pt}
\subsection{Stage 1: Semantic Feature Compression via VAE}

The VAE is trained offline following the standard ELBO
objective~\cite{ref_vae}. The probabilistic formulation is preferred over a
deterministic autoencoder because KL regularization constrains the latent
space toward a smooth Gaussian prior, improving generalization to unseen
channel states and suppressing measurement noise before the RL stage.
At inference, the frozen encoder maps each
raw observation $\mathbf{x}_i(t)\in\mathbb{R}^d$ to a compact
deterministic latent code
$\hat{\mathbf{z}}_i(t)=\bm{\mu}_\phi(\mathbf{x}_i(t))\in\mathbb{R}^{d_z}$,
which serves as the MADRL agent state.

\vspace{-6pt}
\subsection{Stage 2: EKF-Based DT}
\label{sec:dt}

Unlike static classifiers that process each observation in isolation,
the proposed DT-EKF~\cite{ref_ekf} maintains a dynamic estimate of
$\mathbf{s}_i(t)=[{\rm RSSI}_i(t),\,n^{\rm BS}_i(t)]^\top$
in linear mW units instead of the logarithmic dBm scale, so that the additive Gaussian noise assumption holds accurately.
This recursive formulation provides three capabilities unavailable in static methods, namely
(i)~adaptive noise weighting via the time-varying Kalman gain,
(ii)~statistically principled anomaly scoring via the Normalized Innovation Squared (NIS) chi-squared test,
and (iii)~data-adaptive quality threshold via Youden's J.
EKF is preferred over the Unscented Kalman Filter for its lower computational
overhead, since the mild tanh nonlinearity is well-handled by first-order Jacobian
linearization at IoT channel dynamics timescales.

The proposed nonlinear state transition for the RSSI component is
\begin{equation}
s^{(1)}_i(t+1) = s^{(1)}_i(t)
  + \alpha\tanh\!\Bigl(\tfrac{\mu_i - s^{(1)}_i(t)}{\sigma_i}\Bigr)
  + w^{(1)}_t,
\label{eq:ss1}
\end{equation}
where $\mu_i$, $\sigma_i$ are the dataset RSSI mean and standard deviation
and $\alpha{=}0.3$ is the mean-reversion strength, empirically chosen to balance reversion speed and state smoothness. The tanh term drives $s^{(1)}_i(t)$ toward its empirical mean $\mu_i$ with saturation at extreme values, preventing state divergence beyond the physical RSSI boundary. The base-station count follows a standard random walk: $s^{(2)}_i(t+1) = s^{(2)}_i(t) + w^{(2)}_t$, where $w^{(2)}_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_Q^2)$. The observation model is
$\mathbf{o}_i(t)=\mathbf{s}_i(t)+\mathbf{v}(t)$ (identity mapping),
where $\mathbf{o}_i(t)$ is the noisy channel measurement (distinct from
the VAE code $\hat{\mathbf{z}}_i(t)$). A standard KF requires a linear, time-invariant state transition with a constant matrix $\mathbf{F}$. The tanh term violates this assumption: its partial derivative with respect to $s^{(1)}$ is $1 - (\alpha/\sigma_i)\,\mathrm{sech}^2((\mu_i - s^{(1)})/\sigma_i)$, which is state-dependent and therefore changes at every time step. EKF~\cite{ref_ekf} resolves this by analytically computing the Jacobian $\mathbf{F}_t$ at each step through first-order linearization around the prior estimate $\hat{s}^{(1)}_{t|t-1}$, allowing the standard predict-update equations to remain valid under these nonlinear dynamics.

\textit{Anomaly detection.}
The NIS statistic is
\begin{equation}
\xi_t = \mathbf{y}_t^\top \mathbf{S}_t^{-1} \mathbf{y}_t \sim \chi^2(2)
\label{eq:nis}
\end{equation}
under the no-fault hypothesis, where $\mathbf{y}_t$ is the EKF innovation
and $\mathbf{S}_t$ its covariance~\cite{ref_ekf}. Step $t$ is flagged
anomalous if $\xi_t > \chi^2_{0.95}(2) \approx 5.99$, controlling the
false-alarm rate at 5\%.

\textit{Quality classification.}
The EKF-estimated $\hat{s}^{(1)}_{t|t}$ (mW) is converted to dBm as
$\widehat{\rm RSSI}_{t}=10\log_{10}(\hat{s}^{(1)}_{t|t})$, and the optimal
threshold $\tau^*=\arg\max_\tau[\mathrm{TPR}(\tau)-\mathrm{FPR}(\tau)]$
is selected via Youden's J on the EKF output, as motivated in
Section~\ref{sec:model}.

\vspace{-6pt}
\subsection{Stage 3: MADRL-GAT}

\textit{Policy network.}
Agent $i$'s local state at time $t$ is the VAE latent code
$\hat{\mathbf{z}}_i(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d_z}$, where $d_z=16$ for
Sigfox Antwerp and $d_z=3$ for LoRaWAN Italy and Indoor
(see Table~\ref{tab:config}). Agents are connected in graph $\mathcal{G}$ with adjacency matrix
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{A}_{ij} = \begin{cases}
\rho\!\bigl({\rm RSSI}_i,\, {\rm RSSI}_j\bigr), & \text{Antwerp (top-10 BSs)} \\
1, & \text{LoRaWAN, Indoor (full graph)}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:adj}
\end{equation}
where $\rho(\cdot,\cdot)$ is the Pearson correlation over RSSI time series, serving as a co-coverage indicator: agents with correlated RSSI likely share base-station coverage and thus affect each other's resource decisions. Each agent independently selects a discrete action $a_i(t)\in\{1,\ldots,|\mathcal{A}|\}$.
Before Q-value estimation, a two-head GAT~\cite{ref_gat_comm} aggregates each agent's neighbourhood as
\begin{equation}
\tilde{\mathbf{z}}_i = \sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}(i)}\alpha_{ij}\,W\hat{\mathbf{z}}_j,
\quad \alpha_{ij}=\mathrm{softmax}_j\!\bigl(e_{ij}\bigr),
\label{eq:gat}
\end{equation}
where $e_{ij}$ is the attention score between agents $i$ and $j$ and $W$
is a shared linear projection. The attention mechanism learns to assign higher weight to neighbours whose latent states are more relevant to agent $i$'s local channel condition, enabling data-driven inter-agent coordination without manual weight design. Unlike Graph Convolutional Networks that apply fixed normalized aggregation, GAT adapts per-edge weights to heterogeneous IoT topologies where inter-agent relevance varies across deployments.
The aggregated embedding $\tilde{\mathbf{z}}_i$ then feeds the
Q-network $Q_i(\tilde{\mathbf{z}}_i;\bm{\theta}_i)$.
The EKF quality label $\hat{q}_i(t)$ and anomaly flag $\xi_i(t)$ from
Stage~2 enter the learning signal via the reward rather than the state vector,
keeping the state compact.

\textit{DQN training.}
Each agent maintains an independent Q-network $Q_i(\cdot;\bm{\theta}_i)$
trained via the standard DQN~\cite{ref_dqn} Bellman loss with discount factor $\gamma$
and a periodically updated target network $\bm{\theta}_i^-$
to stabilize training. DQN is adopted for its natural compatibility with
discrete action spaces. Policy-gradient alternatives such as PPO
incur higher variance and require more sample interactions under the
short 100-episode training budget. The per-agent reward encodes an adaptive resource trade-off in which
higher resource actions boost effective signal quality when the channel
is weak but always incur a resource cost,
\vspace{-5pt}
\begin{equation}
r_i(t) = 5\,\tilde{s}_i(t) + 2\,\bar{n}_i(t)
        - 3\,\bar{a}(t)
        + \max\!\bigl(0,\,1 - \tfrac{\sigma_a(t)}{2}\bigr),
\label{eq:reward}
\end{equation}
\vspace{-4pt}
where $\bar{s}_i(t)=\mathrm{clip}(({\rm RSSI}_i(t){+}145)/95,0,1)$ normalizes RSSI over $[-145,-50]$~dBm~\cite{ref_iot_overview}, $\tilde{s}_i(t)=\bar{s}_i(t)+\bar{a}(t)(1-\bar{s}_i(t))$ is the action-boosted effective signal with mean action $\bar{a}(t)\in[0,1]$, $\bar{n}_i(t)=\min(n_i^{\rm BS}/20,1)$ is the normalized BS count, and $\sigma_a(t)$ is the cross-agent action standard deviation. The reward is non-negative and bounded above by 8, since $5\tilde{s}_i - 3\bar{a} \geq 0$ holds for all channel states, and boosting resources is beneficial only when the channel is poor (low $\bar{s}_i$).

compression is intrinsically simple given the low input dimensionality, yielding
MSEs of $0.016$ and $0.054$ respectively. In all cases, the compact latent codes reduce the RL state space and remove
measurement noise, accelerating policy convergence compared to learning directly
on high-dimensional raw observations. Rapid convergence reflects the structured sparsity of IoT channel data rather than underfitting.

\subsection{DT-EKF Performance}

Fig.~\ref{fig:dt} confirms the EKF attenuates injected fault disturbances while tracking the channel trend across all datasets. Fig.~\ref{fig:roc} shows that DT-EKF outperforms K-Means and GMM in quality classification on all datasets, with the largest gain on LoRaWAN (+80.4\% AUC) where clustering baselines are near-random. On Indoor, Youden's-J optimization achieves accuracy of 0.934, surpassing K-Means by +16.6 percentage points.

For anomaly detection, Fig.~\ref{fig:roc} shows that IF is the strongest single-task baseline on Antwerp (AUC 0.949) and LoRaWAN (0.992), while LOF is near-random across all datasets. DT-EKF decisively wins on Indoor (AUC 0.987 vs.\ IF 0.896, Z-Score 0.958), because the bivariate NIS statistic jointly exploits complementary fault signatures in both RSSI and base-station count, providing a structural advantage over univariate detectors. Crucially, IF and Z-Score provide no quality-classification capability, whereas DT-EKF simultaneously delivers both outputs within a single recursive filter pass without labeled data.

\vspace{-8pt}
\subsection{Discussion}

\textit{Computational overhead.}
Online inference covers three stages, namely VAE encoder ($\mathcal{O}(d\,d_z)$),
EKF predict-update ($\mathcal{O}(m^2)$, $m{=}2$), and GAT Q-network
($\mathcal{O}(N^2 d_z)$ per head). Per-stage measured latency is detailed in
Table~\ref{tab:runtime}, where the GAT Q-network accounts for the dominant share ($\sim$70--77\%), attributable to per-step tensor allocation overhead rather than arithmetic complexity at the small $N{=}5$ scale. Profiling on constrained edge hardware remains future work.

\textit{Concept drift.}
The offline-trained VAE encoder is sensitive to distribution shift from seasonal variation or hardware aging. This could be addressed by periodically fine-tuning the encoder on buffered recent observations via reconstruction loss. Since DT-EKF and MADRL-GAT operate in the latent space, preserving its structure through encoder-only updates keeps both downstream stages valid without retraining.

\vspace{-8pt}
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% ============================================================

This paper presents a unified three-stage framework for intelligent IoT
resource management that couples VAE-based semantic compression, an
EKF-based DT for joint channel monitoring, and MADRL-GAT for
cooperative resource allocation into a coherent sequential pipeline.
The key design principle is that compressed latent codes serve as both
the agent state and the quality-aware reward input, establishing a
concrete information path from raw sensing to resource decisions.
The DT-EKF is the only evaluated method that simultaneously delivers
quality classification and anomaly detection within a single recursive
filter pass without labeled fault data, outperforming K-Means and GMM
in quality AUC by up to $+80.4\%$ on LoRaWAN and achieving anomaly
AUC of 0.987 on Indoor.
MADRL-GAT converges stably within 100 episodes and achieves a $+4.6\%$
reward gain over DQN-noGAT on the high-dimensional Sigfox Antwerp dataset,
confirming that graph-attention cooperative learning provides measurable advantage when inter-agent graph structure is informative.
Together, these results confirm cross-technology generalizability across
Sigfox, LoRaWAN, and WiFi, extending the scope of prior single-domain evaluations.
Future work will explore Unscented Kalman Filtering for more strongly
nonlinear channel conditions, Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)-based agents for improved sample
efficiency, and federated deployment for privacy-preserving
distributed IoT management.
